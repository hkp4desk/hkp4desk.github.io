Why We Are Starting HKP4 Now

5 November 2026The HKP4 DeskProsperity ProsperityProjection ProjectionProspect ProspectPotential Potential

中文版本 Chinese version →

Hong Kong does not lack commentary. It lacks the discipline of putting commentary back in front of evidence. Starting today, HKP4 observes the city through four lenses, published simultaneously in Traditional Chinese and English.

The four lenses

Prosperity 繁榮 asks the plainest question: where does the money come from, and where does it go? Beyond GDP, it is wages, rents, property prices, and the tally of shops opening and closing that read the real thermometer of prosperity.

Projection 推演 refuses both ends of the market for predictions — neither doom-mongering nor praise. Using only public data and institutional trajectories, we write “if… then…” scenarios precise enough to be judged later by reality. The value of a projection is not in being right, but in being honest: every assumption stated, then left to the verdict of events.

Prospect 前景 watches where policy direction, regional competition, and the tension between the Greater Bay Area and international channels open windows of opportunity — and close them. A prospect is not a wish list; it is a timetable.

Potential 潛能 counts the capabilities still unreleased: professional services, the intermediary role between Chinese and Western capital, the common-law tradition, the trust accumulated over generations. The tragedy of potential is not that it vanishes, but that it is pretended away.

Why anonymous

Because in an age of identity politics, a signed opinion is tried for its author’s identity before its content. We restore the order: only arguments here. The full reasoning — and its honest limits — are on the About page.

Why we write in English too

Half of Hong Kong’s story must be argued inward, in Chinese; the other half must be explained outward, in English. Writing only one language tells only half the truth.

What to expect

One article a week, rotating through the four lenses. Every piece ends with its sources and a “re-check point” for next week — we grade our own projections, and the scores are public.

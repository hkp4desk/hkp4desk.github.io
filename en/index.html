    Eric Chan Should Answer: What the Government Will Do on the Day, and What the Site Will Become

    Eric Chan says lawful mourning is 'absolutely permitted', and the Taoist rites, Buddhist services and Masses are already fixed to the hour. Asked whether the government will hold its own memorial, and how police will treat people who bring flowers to the site, he gave no direct answer. After Wenchuan, 12 May became Disaster Prevention and Reduction Day, turning the most sorrowful day into a day that teaches prevention; after the Jiaozhou Road fire in Shanghai, officials came in person on the seventh day, the lesson was written into rules, and the grief was kept within bounds. What the site becomes should look forward: not only a place of mourning, but a fire-safety education hall that teaches prevention and carries the city forward.

    Joseph Ha: Such a Sensitive Matter Cannot End with a 'Misunderstanding'

    On 15 November the Immaculate Heart of Mary Church in Tai Po held its memorial Mass: a full church, several hundred people, Auxiliary Bishop Joseph Ha blessing the bereaved one by one. The Mass being held is a good thing. But the deleted post and the dropped words were the parish's own doing, and the 'persecution' rumoured outside never existed. Ha's 'I believe it was a misunderstanding' cannot wave the affair away. He owes a consistent account, and an apology: religious freedom is intact, and a police inquiry is a duty, not persecution.

    The Church Is Not Outside the Law: A Mass for the Wang Fuk Court Dead Is Proper, Insinuation Is Not

    Cardinal Stephen Chow asked every parish to offer a Mass on 26 November for the dead of Wang Fuk Court. The prayer then speaks of holding to truth and practising justice. Father Joseph Ha called the letter a direction steadied in the storm. Mourning is entirely proper. Truth and justice with no owner are insinuation. A Father who raises himself on the grief of orphans is a charlatan, not a pastor. The church is protected by law, and it stands inside the law.

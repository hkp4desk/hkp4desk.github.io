    The Church Is Not Outside the Law: A Mass for the Wang Fuk Court Dead Is Proper, Insinuation Is Not

    Cardinal Stephen Chow asked every parish to offer a Mass on 26 November for the dead of Wang Fuk Court. The prayer then speaks of holding to truth and practising justice. Father Joseph Ha called the letter a direction steadied in the storm. Mourning is entirely proper. Truth and justice with no owner are insinuation. A Father who raises himself on the grief of orphans is a charlatan, not a pastor. The church is protected by law, and it stands inside the law.

    The Lai and Wong Cases: Which Step of Asking a Foreign Power to Sanction Your Own Country Was Democratic? The Concept-Swapping Show Has to End

    Jimmy Lai drew 20 years, Joshua Wong 10, and within a week of sentencing more than a dozen voices, from international groups and Western politicians to exiled op-edists, recast the security-law cases as a blow to democracy. We lay their own words next to the judgments and the West's own precedents: those who swap the concept recast a trial of conduct as a trial of opinion. Democracy argues inside the house; collusion works outside it.
